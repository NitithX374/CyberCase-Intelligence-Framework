\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) can summarise cybercrime complaints that span several documents, but an
investigator cannot act on a summary without knowing which sentence of which document supports
each statement, and citations written by the model are as fragile as the rest of its output. We
present CyberCase, a case-analysis system that moves traceability from the prompt into the
backend. Case documents are partitioned into Source units with exact offsets and stable
identifiers; a Reader LLM may only \emph{select} identifiers when writing a Claim, and the
backend reproduces the quoted text. A frozen Claim/Source check (multilingual sentence selection,
NLI and a logistic decision boundary) then labels each Claim, and every summary sentence must end
with the identifiers of the Claims it rests on. On WiCE the check improves Macro-F1 over
thresholded NLI by 0.033 (95\% CI 0.013--0.053). On AttributionBench, machine translation into
Thai raises its false acceptance by 17.4 points in-domain and 4.4 out-of-domain. In a paired
replay, using the check as a hard gate cut the share of unsupported benchmark Claims cited in
summaries from 100\% to 22.2\% ($p=0.016$) but withheld 31\% of supported Claims, so the deployed
system shows the verdict as a warning instead. Accuracy on native Thai case files remains
unmeasured.

\keywords{Evidence traceability \and Large language models \and Claim verification \and
Natural language inference \and Cybercrime investigation \and Thai language}
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

Cybercrime complaints rarely arrive as one clean narrative. A typical case holds a victim's
account, screenshots of chats, bank transfer slips and scanned police forms, much of it in Thai
and much of it recovered by optical character recognition (OCR). LLMs can condense such a case
into a readable summary, but they also produce fluent statements that no source supports
\cite{ji2023hallucination}. In an investigation, an unsupported amount, date or name is not a
stylistic flaw: it can send an officer after the wrong account.

The common remedy is to make the model cite. Retrieval-augmented generation conditions the
output on retrieved passages \cite{lewis2020rag}, and attributed-generation methods train or
prompt models to attach citations or quoted spans to each sentence
\cite{gao2023alce,slobodkin2024afg}. Domain workflows go further and label every output unit
with an explicit evidence identifier \cite{yang2026traceable}. In all of these, however, the
pointer is still text written by the model, so its existence and accuracy must be checked
afterwards, often by people.

CyberCase takes a different position: \emph{the model chooses evidence; the backend owns it}.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An analysis architecture in which a claims-only Reader selects backend-issued Source-unit
identifiers, the backend resolves them to original spans with document and page locators, and a
separate Judgement stage writes a summary over Claims alone, ending each sentence with Claim
identifiers (Sect.~\ref{sec:system}).
\item A frozen Claim/Source check, B1-LR, built from off-the-shelf multilingual models and a
three-weight logistic boundary, evaluated on WiCE, on AttributionBench, and on a Thai machine
translation of AttributionBench (Sect.~\ref{sec:eval}).
\item A paired downstream replay that quantifies the coverage cost of using such a check as a
filter, and the resulting design decision to deploy it as a warning label (Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}
The setting is a public-safety service where trustworthiness has to be inspectable: every
statement an officer reads should lead back to a document, a page and a character range.

%======================================================================
\section{Related Work}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Attributed generation and its evaluation.}
Attribution is commonly defined as whether a statement is supported by an identified source
\cite{rashkin2023ais}. ALCE benchmarks LLMs that generate text with citations
\cite{gao2023alce}; Attribute First, then Generate selects source spans before planning and
writing sentences \cite{slobodkin2024afg}; FActScore decomposes long outputs into atomic facts
and checks each against a knowledge source \cite{min2023factscore}. AttributionBench shows that
automatic attribution evaluation remains hard even for fine-tuned models
\cite{li2024attributionbench}, and WiCE provides real-world claim/evidence entailment labels at
sub-sentence granularity \cite{kamoi2023wice}. We reuse the last two as evaluation data rather
than proposing a new attribution model.

\paragraph{Traceable workflows in professional domains.}
Worledge et al.\ decompose default-judgment review into legal sub-requirements, ground each in
quotes copied from the court file, and measure faster and more accurate human review
\cite{worledge2026default}. Dehing et al.\ generate structured actor, role and timeline reports
from chat evidence with trace identifiers for digital forensics \cite{dehing2026forensics}.
Yang et al.\ register exhibition evidence under stable identifiers and label every generated
script unit as grounded or creative, auditing coverage manually with two experts
\cite{yang2026traceable}. CyberCase shares the unit-level identifier idea but enforces it
mechanically: identifiers are issued and resolved by the backend, invented identifiers are
rejected, and a model-based check runs on every Claim without human annotation at runtime.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the flow. A \emph{case} owns one or more sources: narratives
typed by the user and text extracted from uploaded documents, where every PDF page is rendered
and read by OCR and page locators are kept. The system writes in Thai whenever any source
contains a Thai character.

\paragraph{Source units.}
At analysis time each source is partitioned into units that reuse sentence boundaries (Thai
sentence segmentation with PyThaiNLP's CRF model; table rows become their own units) and preserve
all separators. Every unit
satisfies $\mathit{text} = \mathit{source}[\mathit{start}{:}\mathit{end}]$, and the ordered units
reconstruct the source exactly. A canonical identifier has the form
\texttt{source\_id:U001-}$h$, where $h$ is 16 hexadecimal characters derived from the
segmentation version and the full source text, so an identifier issued for one revision of a
document cannot silently bind to another.

\paragraph{Gap assessment before expensive work.}
A cheap first LLM call checks the case against a fixed 5W1H checklist (who was affected, who was
responsible, what, when, where, why, how and how much). A deterministic policy then either asks the
user one focused question and stops, or proceeds. Rounds and questions per round are bounded,
and answers are stored as conversation messages with their own identifiers (\texttt{QA-01}),
not as case sources.

\paragraph{Claims-only Reading.}
The Reader receives all sources with compact local identifiers (\texttt{U001}, \dots) and returns a
strict JSON object with a version and a list of Claims. Each Claim has an identifier, a type, its
text, an epistemic status (\texttt{reported} for attributed assertions, \texttt{unknown} for
uncertainty stated in a source) and supporting and contradicting unit identifiers. The Reader
cannot emit quotations, reasoning or metadata. Every model stage is validated locally against a
strict schema and is retried at most once on invalid or truncated output.

\paragraph{Backend binding.}
Local identifiers are mapped back to canonical ones, then checked for syntax, source ownership,
existence, stale hashes and duplicates. Only then does the backend reproduce the original text,
with offsets, document identity and page locator. An unresolvable identifier is recorded as an
invalid pointer rather than replaced by model text. Consequently, every quotation that appears
in the analysis view or the report is a verbatim substring of a stored source. We call a Claim
\emph{bound} when its supporting citations resolve; bound describes addressability, not truth.

\paragraph{Claim/Source check (B1-LR).}
For each bound Claim whose epistemic status permits assessment, a frozen multilingual MPNet
encoder \cite{reimers2020multilingual} scores the cosine similarity between the Claim and each
resolved supporting unit. Units with similarity $\ge 0.20$ are retained in original order (the
first maximum if none pass) and joined with single newlines into a premise. A multilingual
mDeBERTa-v3 NLI model \cite{he2021debertav3,laurer2022mdeberta} scores the premise/Claim pair
with longest-first truncation at 512 tokens, giving probabilities
$(p_E, p_N, p_C)$. A logistic regression fitted once on the WiCE training split
\cite{kamoi2023wice} maps them to a score
\[
s = \sigma\!\left(1.411\,p_E - 1.051\,p_N - 0.302\,p_C + 0.058\right),
\]
and $s \ge 0.5$ yields \texttt{supported}, otherwise \texttt{not\_supported}. Claims with
unresolved or missing support, declared conflicts or uncertain status are left
\texttt{unassessed}. All selection indices, similarities, NLI probabilities, scores and
truncation flags are stored with the Claim. The score is a task-specific decision value, not a
calibrated probability that the Claim is true.

\paragraph{Judgement, views and reports.}
Judgement receives each Claim's identifier, type, text, status and citation text, plus the
follow-up history, but never the case sources directly, the verifier verdict, or external
threat-intelligence context. It returns a summary in which every sentence ends with bracketed
Claim identifiers, and the request states a sentence limit of one per three Claims (between two and
eight). Unknown identifiers in the summary are rejected. A separate, concurrent LLM call extracts
parties, timeline and impacts from the same Claims for display only; its failure records a warning
without failing the analysis. Reports are deterministic snapshots of the stored analysis,
rendered to HTML and PDF without any further model call. An optional retrieval service supplies
MITRE ATT\&CK context when an applicability gate requests it; it is never treated as case
evidence and is outside the scope of this paper's evaluation.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
%======================================================================

We ask three questions. \textbf{RQ1}: does the B1-LR decision rule improve on thresholded NLI?
\textbf{RQ2}: how does the frozen check behave when inputs are machine-translated into Thai?
\textbf{RQ3}: what happens downstream when its verdict controls which Claims reach Judgement?
No model was trained or tuned on test data; all components were frozen before the test runs.

\subsection{RQ1: Verifier Design on WiCE}

WiCE provides claim/evidence pairs from Wikipedia with entailment labels
\cite{kamoi2023wice}; we evaluate on the held-out test split ($N=1{,}070$; 330 supported,
740 not). Design choices (selector, threshold, decision head, backbone) were made on the
development split. We compare: \textbf{B0}, the probability of entailment on the full evidence
with a development-tuned threshold; \textbf{B1}, the same rule on the selected units;
\textbf{B1-LR}, the deployed rule; and \textbf{B3}, which adds reverse-direction NLI features to
the logistic head. Uncertainty uses 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples.

acceptance rate, the share of gold-unsupported pairs labelled supported. Per-Claim latency on a GTX~1650 is about
390\,ms for B0, 227\,ms for B1 and B1-LR, and 449\,ms for B3.}
\label{tab:wice}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}

\textbf{B1-LR: selected units, LR} & \textbf{66.64} & \textbf{.618} & \textbf{46.26} & \textbf{50.61} & \textbf{26.22} \\

Table~\ref{tab:wice} shows that selection alone raises supported recall but also raises false
acceptance; adding the logistic boundary recovers precision. B1-LR improves Macro-F1 over B0
by 0.033 (95\% CI 0.013--0.053) and supported F1 by 0.058 (0.029--0.087), with no significant
change in false acceptance ($+0.75$ pp, CI $-1.09$ to $+2.70$). B3 is statistically
indistinguishable from B1-LR (Macro-F1 $-0.002$, CI $-0.011$ to $+0.007$) at roughly twice the
latency, so B1-LR was kept. The absolute level is modest: about one in four gold-unsupported pairs is
still accepted.

\subsection{RQ2: Transfer to Machine-Translated Thai}

We use the balanced AttributionBench test sets \cite{li2024attributionbench}: 1,610 in-domain
(ID) and 1,686 out-of-domain (OOD) claim/reference rows. All 33,583 unique claim and reference
texts were translated English$\to$Thai with Google Cloud Translation (NMT model), keeping row
order, unit segmentation and gold labels. Both languages were rescored with identical frozen
assets. Thai labels are \emph{inherited} from English under a label-invariance assumption; no
bilingual human adjudication was performed.

Table~\ref{tab:transfer} shows why a single F1 value is misleading here. In-domain F1 is
unchanged (.634 in both languages), yet the Thai condition accepts 140 more unsupported rows and
recovers 65 more supported ones: the operating point shifts toward permissiveness. Out of domain,
Thai lowers F1 by 0.040 (CI 0.018--0.063) and raises false acceptance by 4.4 points. For a check whose
purpose is to warn about unsupported Claims, the rise in false acceptance is the result that
matters. As a diagnostic, multilingual BGE-M3 embeddings place each English text close to its
translation (mean cosine 0.846), which says nothing about whether names, amounts and negations
survived translation.

\subsection{RQ3: Downstream Replay with the Check as a Gate}

To see what the check changes downstream, we replayed the Judgement stage on AttributionBench
rather than on cases, because only there do Claims carry gold labels. Twenty label-blind,
hash-ordered clusters (10 ID, 10 OOD; 60 rows, 29 attributable and 31 not) were each turned into a
virtual case whose Claims are the benchmark claims and whose sources are their references. The
same Claims, identifiers and prompt were sent to Judgement under two conditions: \emph{no gate}
(every Claim admitted) and \emph{B1-LR gate} (only \texttt{supported} Claims admitted, using the
frozen decisions). Judgement used Gemma~4~26B with temperature 0 and fixed providers. The outcome is
whether a non-attributable row's Claim identifier is cited in the generated summary.

As a gate, B1-LR lowered the share of unsupported Claim identifiers cited in summaries from
100\% to 22.2\% (paired difference $-77.8$ pp, cluster-bootstrap CI $-95.2$ to $-53.3$). Seven
cluster pairs lost unsupported citations and none gained them (exact McNemar $p=0.016$). The cost is
visible in the same table: 9 of 29 supported Claims were withheld, and four clusters had no
admitted Claim at all, so no summary was produced. Of the 36 Judgement calls (four gated clusters needed none), six failed by exhausting
the output budget during model reasoning; we report those pairs as unknown rather than safe and
exclude them from the citation counts. Judgement latency was not reduced (mean 114\,s without the gate,
169\,s with it, including failures).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Gate or label.}
The replay shows a real trade-off: a filter that removes about three quarters of unsupported Claims
from summaries also removes about three in ten supported ones, and the Thai results suggest that the
filter would be leakier on Thai input. For an investigator, a silently missing true fact can be as
costly as a flagged dubious one. CyberCase therefore deploys the verdict as a warning label: every
Claim that passes the structural checks reaches Judgement, a \texttt{not\_supported} Claim is shown
with a plain warning in the analysis view and the report, the verdict never
enters a model prompt, and if the verifier models are missing the Claims are marked unassessed instead
of failing the analysis. The structural guarantees stay strict: invented,
stale or duplicate identifiers are still rejected, and no generated quotation is ever displayed.

\paragraph{What is and is not established.}
The structural properties follow from construction and are covered by the system's automated
tests. The semantic results are benchmark results. We have no native Thai case files with gold
support labels, and no human annotation of any kind: Thai labels are inherited through machine
translation, WiCE and AttributionBench are English Wikipedia and web QA data, and the replay uses
benchmark claims rather than claims written by our Reader. We therefore do not claim accuracy on
real complaints, factual or legal correctness, reduced hallucination in prose, or any benefit to
investigators. The replay sample is small and purposive. We also observed one summary that
introduced a population figure absent from all Claim texts but present in a cited passage, a reminder
that citation text reaching Judgement is an exposure path that Claim-level checks do not close.
Finally, mDeBERTa was chosen on WiCE development data before testing; a smaller multilingual MiniLM
backbone, run afterwards as a sensitivity check, scored higher on WiCE test (Macro-F1 .650 vs .618),
so we do not claim the deployed backbone is optimal.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
The most important missing evidence is a Thai benchmark built from real, anonymised complaints.
Without large-scale annotation, we plan to combine labels known by construction (Claims copied from
a source versus Claims with altered amounts, dates, roles or negation), metamorphic tests on live
analyses (removing or swapping a cited unit should lower the score), the human-translated Thai test
set of XNLI for the NLI component, and a small doubly annotated subset to report inter-annotator
agreement and to validate any LLM judge used at scale. A claims-text-only Judgement ablation would
measure how much citation text contributes to unsupported additions.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================

CyberCase shows that evidence traceability for LLM case analysis can be enforced by the system
rather than requested from the model: Source units with stable identifiers, a Reader that may only
select them, backend reproduction of every quotation, and summaries whose sentences point to Claims.
A frozen Claim/Source check adds a semantic signal for about 0.23\,s per Claim on a consumer GPU
(about 1\,s on CPU), but our
measurements show both its value as a filter and its cost in coverage and Thai robustness, which led
us to show it as a warning rather than let it decide. Establishing how well this works on real Thai
complaints is the next step.
